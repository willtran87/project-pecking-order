# Reward and personal-goal polish

This follow-up resolves the actionable product changes from the ten-point review. It does not treat automated checks as evidence that people find the game fun.

1. **A consistent opening.** Route-first coaching and accessible instructions describe automatic production, with optional care and Peck lessons excluded from the required funnel. Legacy saved induction still uses its original steps.
2. **A simpler reward choice.** One spendable balance leads the screen. Equipment cards disclose final matched prices; exact reserve/match arithmetic remains available through accessible details. Banking explicitly forfeits the unused match.
3. **Honest pacing evidence.** First production has a 45-tick regression budget (33.75 simulation seconds after desk arrival). Local telemetry separately records routing-to-laying, laying-to-physical-delivery, and total elapsed time against a 45-second target. Pauses and rendering delays count; slow software-rendered browser timing is not represented as a successful real-player timing study.
4. **Readable attention.** Safe overview states say ALL GOOD, not WORKING. A selected hen distinguishes paused, resting, waiting for work, and actual progress, while needs retain their actionable cause.
5. **One optional lesson.** After reinvestment, a short status hint points to Playbook's replayable check-in lesson. It opens existing care choices without spending, stopping work, or requiring a timed Peck.
6. **Direct world controls.** Clicking an intent marker opens its relevant existing action. Clicking a desk monitor opens equipment prices. Neither automatically buys anything; equipment/intent inspection also cannot commit a pending route by accident. Tutorial and modal input boundaries remain in force.
7. **Causal reactions.** A newly filed specialty match gets a named-hen bark and concise consequence. Existing assist and recovery effects remain attached to their real receipts rather than synthetic bonuses.
8. **Equipment before/after.** The first newly produced egg after a purchase carries the kit's before/after speed, crack-risk reduction, or strain reduction, respecting the comfort cap. Risk reduction is stated in percentage points and never promises a perfect shell. Older in-flight eggs cannot claim the demonstration.
9. **An accepted next-shift goal.** Review offers one optional, cancelable goal based on actual cracks, late files, or output. Accepted goals persist in the existing campaign save, show factual progress in an existing HUD slot, and celebrate only when the agreed shift satisfies the conditions. Safety goals require meeting quota. There is no additional currency, purchase, penalty, or promised payout.
10. **Five-player study remains pending.** The updated session card covers reward affordability, automatic work, optional lessons, equipment causality, goal acceptance, and voluntary continuation. Real participants and their observations must still be supplied.

## Persistence and verification

The optional goal has its own validated version-1 payload. Older saves need no migration; malformed goal data is discarded without invalidating an otherwise valid career. Native coverage exercises offer derivation, JSON normalization, settlement, cancellation, actual save staging/activation, price previews, and non-purchasing equipment navigation. Existing route-first, legacy onboarding, economic, UI, and lifecycle regressions remain in the release gate.
